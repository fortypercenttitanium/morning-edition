# Editorial brief — The Morning Edition

Produce a real, varied morning newspaper, not a collection of personalized hobby notifications.

## Reader and place
- Based in Medford / Burlington County, South Jersey; local reporting also covers Philadelphia and New Jersey.
- Reading target: 15–20 minutes, a serious-but-accessible newspaper.
- Prioritize genuinely newsworthy events and independent judgment rather than filling fixed quotas.

## Core mix
- US and world: politics, major policy, economy, significant international events; balanced breadth without monopolizing the paper.
- Local: civic issues, schools, government, zoning and development, transport, local business, noteworthy events, community life, Philadelphia.
- Sports: regular Philadelphia pro-team coverage and meaningful national NFL, MLB, NBA, NHL coverage, with verified dates/results.
- Technology and industry: software engineering, AI, engineering, emerging technologies, science and policy.
- Science/curiosity: space, history, archaeology, inventions, environment, energy, climate, education, economic trends.
- Culture and games: significant announcements, notable releases, or industry shifts. Do NOT include daily World of Warcraft/Overwatch updates or other routine hobby stories.
- A couple of delightful, surprising stories not obviously related to the reader's established interests.

## Writing and curation
- Major stories: about 2–3 short paragraphs, plus occasional 'Why it matters'. Others: concise briefs.
- Explain politics fairly, including why something matters and possible consequences; no partisan advocacy or false equivalences.
- Distinguish verified facts, allegations, forecasts and analysis. Do not invent facts, scores, event times, dates or sources.
- Link original reporting, preferably freely accessible; paywalled sources are fine if labeled in copy.
- Prefer genuinely fresh developments from the prior ~24–48 hours; longer historical/science features can be older if interesting, and should be clearly dated.
- Do not include stories just because they appeared in yesterday's paper; no old story repeats, recycled 'long reads', or superficial rewrites.
- If a continuing story has an important NEW development since previous coverage, include it with a clearly labeled 'UPDATE' and open with the precise new fact.

## Layout
- Masthead, publication date, lead story, sections, short items, curiosity page, and 2–3 optional longer reads.
- About 18–24 distinct stories if news quality justifies it, but quality over quotas.
