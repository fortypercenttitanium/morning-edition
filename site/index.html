National Politics

Democrats clinch House control after North Carolina recount

A final House race call in North Carolina completed a midterm turnaround that gives Democrats control of both chambers when the next Congress convenes in January.

The Associated Press reported Sunday that Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican Rep. Virginia Foxx after a recount in North Carolina, a result that capped broader Democratic gains in the Nov. 3 midterm elections and secured House control alongside the party’s Senate majority. The outcome turns what had been an extended period of uncertainty in a handful of close races into a clearer governing picture for Washington.

The shift matters immediately for committee control, investigations and the fate of legislation awaiting the next Congress. It also lands as President Donald Trump tries to steady his position after the elections while managing foreign-policy tensions abroad and continued domestic debate over the war with Iran.

Trump says Iran wants a deal, but not one he would accept

The president’s latest comments signaled no near-term diplomatic breakthrough despite efforts to prevent a wider renewed conflict.

President Donald Trump said Friday that Tehran wants a deal to end hostilities but that the terms on offer are not acceptable to him, AP reported. The remarks came amid continued concern in Washington and Gulf capitals that fighting could return to a broader phase even after Trump earlier suggested the war was militarily over for now.

Climate

Update U.N. climate talks end without a final deal

Negotiators left Antalya without agreement after disputes over fossil fuels and broader responsibility sharing.

The U.N. climate conference in Antalya, Turkey, ended without a final agreement Sunday, according to AP, after negotiators failed to bridge divisions over fossil fuels and the obligations of major emitters. The collapse leaves this year’s summit without the kind of consensus document that often frames the next round of national climate bargaining.

The breakdown followed days of friction over whether countries would explicitly commit to moving away from fossil fuels and how much pressure should fall on wealthy economies versus large developing ones. AP reported that the United States did not take part in the talks this year under the Trump administration, adding to the strain around the negotiations.

European governments describe alleged Russian plot targeting a Ukrainian American

Officials in Poland and Denmark said they had disrupted an operation on NATO territory.

AP reported Sunday that Denmark expelled a Russian official after authorities said they had thwarted an alleged plot to kill a Ukrainian American citizen on Danish soil. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk had earlier disclosed an alleged Russian-backed plot in Warsaw, describing it as the first known attempt of its kind against a U.S. citizen on another NATO country’s territory.

The allegations underscore how the war’s risks increasingly spill beyond Ukraine itself. European officials used the case to argue that Russian pressure tactics are reaching deeper into allied territory.

War

Ukraine says naval drones struck a Russian warship in Libya

Kyiv described the attack as another long-range blow against Russian military assets beyond the main front.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Monday that Ukrainian naval drones struck and sank one of Russia’s newer warships in Libyan waters, according to AP. Ukraine also said it hit a Russian cargo vessel used to transport weapons and destroyed a military helicopter in the same operation.

If confirmed in fuller detail, the strike would represent an unusually far-flung extension of Ukraine’s campaign against Russian military logistics and prestige targets. AP reported that a separate sea-drone strike later hit a Maltese-flagged commercial vessel off Benghazi, according to the Libyan coast guard, underscoring the widening maritime risks around the conflict.

World shares mixed as tech weakness lingers

U.S. futures rose early Monday, but pressure on big technology stocks remained a central market theme.

World markets were mixed early Monday while U.S. stock futures moved higher, AP reported, even as large technology shares continued to weigh on sentiment in parts of Asia. Oil prices eased from recent highs but remained well above levels seen before the Iran war began earlier this year.

The early trading snapshot suggested investors were still balancing two competing signals: some stabilization after a bruising stretch for equities, and continuing anxiety about energy prices, geopolitics and the durability of the technology trade that had powered markets for much of the year.

Transit

SEPTA begins adding real-time subway arrival displays

The system is bringing live train information to platforms in a practical service upgrade.

The Philadelphia Inquirer reported this past week that SEPTA is rolling out real-time arrival information to subway platforms, a modest but tangible improvement in daily rider information. The change aims to give passengers clearer timing on train arrivals rather than relying solely on static schedules or app access.

For riders, the benefit is straightforward: more certainty on platforms and quicker notice when service gaps open. The upgrade also reflects SEPTA’s broader effort to modernize basic rider-facing systems even as funding and reliability remain recurring issues.

Update Philadelphia election board meets Monday to certify results

The city’s next formal post-election step follows earlier canvassing and comes after voters approved two charter questions this month.

Philadelphia’s election board was scheduled to meet Monday to certify city results from the Nov. 3 election, according to WHYY’s election coverage and city reporting in recent days. The certification meeting is the formal step that follows canvassing and resolves the city’s role before results move into the broader state process.

This year’s local ballot included two Philadelphia Home Rule Charter questions, both of which voters approved by wide margins, WHYY previously reported: one allowing preferences for small local businesses in city contracting and another making Creative Philadelphia a permanent city office.

City Hall

Update Philadelphia trash-contract fight moves toward full Council vote

A committee stripped the Chester incinerator operator Reworld from the city’s proposed waste-disposal package.

A Philadelphia City Council committee has rejected including Reworld, the company that burns much of the city’s trash in Chester, in a new waste-disposal contract package, WHYY reported Thursday. The amendment removed Reworld from the bill while allowing authorizations for other disposal arrangements to advance.

The move sharpens a long-running fight over where Philadelphia should send its trash after current contracts expire at year’s end. WHYY reported that full Council could take up the bill as early as Dec. 3, with critics arguing the city should move away from incineration and supporters of the broader package warning about the practical limits of disposal capacity.

A City Council hearing examined oversight breakdowns after the death of 10-year-old Malayshia Allford.

Philadelphia City Council held a hearing Thursday on the Department of Human Services and shortcomings in the foster-care system after the death of 10-year-old Malayshia Allford, WHYY reported. OpenAI introduces GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol

The company says the new API models improve reasoning, coding and cost efficiency over their predecessors.
