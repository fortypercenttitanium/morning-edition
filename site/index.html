Thanksgiving nor’easter leaves flooding, outages and travel disruption across the Philadelphia region

A powerful coastal storm canceled Philadelphia’s Thanksgiving parade, flooded shore communities and knocked out power as holiday travel entered its busiest stretch.

A Thanksgiving nor’easter swept through Pennsylvania, New Jersey and Delaware on Thursday, bringing damaging wind, heavy rain and major coastal flooding. The storm forced the cancellation of Philadelphia’s Thanksgiving Day parade, produced record tidal flooding in parts of the shore region and left tens of thousands without electricity around the region.

The storm also disrupted transportation. High winds damaged construction equipment on the Commodore Barry Bridge, prompting a closure expected to last into Saturday. Earlier forecasts had warned of gusts as high as 75 mph near the coast and 55 to 65 mph inland, conditions that also contributed to flight delays and widespread local travel problems during the holiday.

New Jersey went into Thanksgiving under a state emergency declaration ahead of the coastal storm, and the impacts Thursday validated those warnings as flooding hit shore communities and damaging winds spread inland. Atlantic City, Cape May and parts of the Delaware Bay were among the areas facing major to near-historic flooding concerns before landfall.

Trump attacks revised GDP figures and demands a Commerce Department investigation

The president called pre-election growth numbers “fake” after a government revision, renewing pressure on a statistical process that is normally revised on a fixed schedule.

President Donald Trump said Thursday that newly revised economic figures showed the pre-election GDP numbers had been “fake” and demanded an investigation of the Commerce Department. The revisions followed the government’s normal scheduled process, which routinely updates earlier estimates as more complete data becomes available.

The dispute puts fresh political attention on economic data at a moment when inflation, consumer sentiment and holiday spending are under close watch. Trump has argued that stronger growth validates his economic agenda, while critics say his attack on official statistics risks undercutting confidence in routine federal measurements.

Argentina bars central bank from printing money for the government

President Javier Milei’s latest economic overhaul targets a practice long associated with inflation and fiscal instability.

Argentina has formally barred its central bank from financing government spending by printing money, a significant policy change in a country where monetary expansion has repeatedly fed inflation crises. The measure also strips out employment and development goals that had been added under previous Peronist governments.

The move deepens Milei’s market-oriented restructuring as the economy remains under strain. Official data cited by AP showed Argentina’s economy contracted in the second and third quarters, underscoring the political risk around reforms that supporters say are necessary and opponents say are worsening hardship.

Spain heads into an election shaped by housing, migration and energy

An early national vote has become a test of affordability politics in one of Europe’s largest economies.

Spain’s election campaign has centered on housing costs, migration and the country’s energy model, with consequences that extend beyond Madrid into broader European politics. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez called the early election while trying to frame the race around affordability, an issue that has become politically volatile across the continent.

Spain also enters the vote with a relatively strong renewable-energy profile compared with many European peers. That has helped make the election a useful barometer for how cost-of-living pressures interact with migration and climate policy in a major eurozone country.

SEPTA’s real-time information push expands on the street while subway modernization is still being scoped

Agency materials show new rider-facing displays in pilot locations and a separate request for information on heavy-rail real-time systems.

SEPTA has been rolling out pilot ePaper arrival displays at selected bus, trolley and station locations, including Stadium Station and several high-transfer stops, as part of a broader effort to improve rider information. Agency materials say the devices provide arrival predictions, alerts and accessibility features including text-to-speech.

At the same time, SEPTA has sought industry proposals for modernizing real-time passenger information on the Market-Frankford and Broad Street lines. That means surface-level rider information is moving first while the heavier subway-system upgrade remains in the planning stage.

NASA previews another cargo launch to the International Space Station for Sunday

A Cygnus resupply mission is set to carry science investigations, supplies and equipment to the orbital laboratory.

NASA said media events were being prepared for a Sunday, Nov. 29, cargo launch to the International Space Station, with a Northrop Grumman Cygnus spacecraft scheduled to fly on a SpaceX Falcon 9. The agency said the mission will deliver science investigations, supplies and equipment to the station.

The launch would continue a busy stretch of station logistics after a Dragon cargo return earlier this month. NASA also said Nov. 17 that its NICER telescope had ended science operations after more than six years of observing the universe from the International Space Station. The instrument had been used for studies of neutron stars, black holes and other high-energy astrophysical targets.
