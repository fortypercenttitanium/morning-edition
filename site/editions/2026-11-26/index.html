Weather

Powerful Thanksgiving storm lashes the East Coast as Philadelphia parade drops balloons

Forecasters warned of heavy rain, strong winds and coastal flooding from the Mid-Atlantic into New England on Thanksgiving morning, disrupting travel and forcing changes to holiday events.

A strong coastal storm was expected to bring heavy rain, damaging wind gusts and significant coastal flooding to the Eastern Seaboard on Thursday, with federal forecasters warning that Thanksgiving morning travel, parades and outdoor events could be affected from the Carolinas through southern New England. NOAA’s Weather Prediction Center said the system would track up the coast during the holiday, while local forecasters in the Philadelphia region warned of soaking rain and the risk of downed trees and power outages.

In Philadelphia, the 107th Thanksgiving Day Parade was still scheduled to proceed Thursday morning, but organizers said the giant helium balloons would not fly because of the wind forecast. The route and a broad set of road closures remained in place across Center City and the Benjamin Franklin Parkway. At the Shore, Atlantic City officials warned of flooding around the morning high tide as the storm pushed water toward the coast.

NASA awards contract work for NOAA’s next-generation orbiting weather network

The federal agencies said Northrop Grumman won work on microwave sounder instruments for the NEON satellite program.

NASA said Monday that, acting on behalf of NOAA, it had awarded Northrop Grumman a contract for Sounder for Microwave-Based Applications instruments on NOAA’s Near Earth Orbit Network satellites. The award is part of the government’s long-term effort to refresh core weather-observation infrastructure in low Earth orbit.

The NEON program is meant to support future forecasting capacity by replacing aging space-based capabilities with newer instruments. Contracts like this tend to draw limited public attention at first, but they shape the quality and continuity of federal weather data for years.

This week’s sky note: November opened a run of three straight supermoons

NASA’s monthly skywatching guide said the full moon on Nov. 24 was the first in a sequence continuing through winter.

NASA’s monthly skywatching guide noted that the full moon of Nov. 24 was a supermoon, the first in a run of three consecutive months in which the full moon appears slightly larger and brighter than average because of the Moon’s distance from Earth. The agency encouraged viewers to look east around sunset to catch the effect, though it noted the visual difference is subtle.

The guide also bundled the supermoon with a month that included the Leonids, Taurids and a close Mars-Jupiter pairing. It was published earlier in November, but the timing of the full moon made it newly relevant this week.
