Climate summit runs into overtime as delegates haggle over a final deal

Negotiators at the U.N. climate conference in Turkey pushed past the scheduled close, with work crews already packing up parts of the venue while delegates argued over the language and ambition of a final agreement.

Talks at the COP31 summit in Antalya stretched into Saturday after countries failed to finish a closing deal on schedule. Associated Press reported that negotiators were still wrestling with how the world should respond to intensifying warming, including the balance between emissions cuts, adaptation and support for countries already hit by floods, heat and other disasters.

The overtime session underscored how climate diplomacy has become harder even as the physical impacts of warming grow more visible. The next annual summit is already set for Addis Ababa in November 2027, but the immediate question in Antalya was whether delegates could salvage a consensus text rather than leave with a watered-down statement or open fractures among major blocs.
