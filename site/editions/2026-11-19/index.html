Trump, Xi and Putin meet in rare trilateral session as Democrats expand Senate majority

The three leaders gathered in Shenzhen to discuss nuclear risks, Ukraine, Iran and trade friction, while in Washington Democrats’ Senate gains and new Fed minutes signaled a busy political and economic stretch ahead.

President Donald Trump met Thursday in Shenzhen with Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in the first such three-way meeting of the leaders of the United States, China and Russia. AP reported that the session came after separate Trump-Xi talks and was framed by the White House as an attempt to press both rivals on nuclear risks and regional wars.

Early reporting from the meeting said nuclear arms, the wars involving Iran and Ukraine, and unresolved trade disputes were high on the agenda. AP also reported that U.S. officials said China had not fully met commitments made at a previous Trump-Xi meeting on agricultural purchases and rare-earth trade.

Meanwhile, AP reported Wednesday that Democrats flipped nine Senate seats in the 2026 midterms, giving the party a 56-44 majority in the next Congress. The gains came even as midterms usually punish the president’s party, sharpening the pressure on both parties ahead of year-end fiscal and policy fights.

Minutes released Wednesday from the Federal Reserve’s October meeting showed that nearly all officials expected a quarter-point rate increase in December, with some participants having favored a move sooner and two floating a larger half-point step next month. The discussion adds to signs that policymakers believe inflation remains stubborn enough to require tighter policy into early 2027.

COP31 and winter outlooks reflect climate pressure and energy concerns

Delegates in Turkey are wrestling with climate finance and energy insecurity as federal forecasters say El Niño remains central to the winter pattern.

At COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, delegates entered the summit’s final week facing a difficult mix of climate diplomacy and energy insecurity. AP reported that many American governors, mayors and Democratic officials attended even as the Trump administration stayed away from the official U.S. role.

A separate AP report on energy and climate said the International Energy Agency sees no return to prewar energy conditions after the Iran conflict’s shock, tying immediate fuel security worries to the longer transition debate.

NOAA said in its winter outlook last month that El Niño was likely to be a major driver of U.S. weather in December through February, favoring warmer-than-average temperatures across much of the northern United States and wetter-than-average conditions across the southern tier and the Eastern Seaboard. The agency said its next update would be available Nov. 19.

Council committee rejects proposed Philadelphia contract with Chester incinerator operator

A City Council committee removed Reworld from a waste-disposal contract package after opposition from Chester residents and environmental advocates.

A Philadelphia City Council committee on Wednesday refused to advance a proposed new contract with Reworld Waste, the company that burns much of Philadelphia’s trash at its incinerator in Chester. WHYY reported that council members amended the ordinance to strike Reworld from a broader authorization for three sanitation contracts before sending the bill onward.

The fight has become a regional environmental-justice issue because Chester has long borne the burden of heavy industrial uses, including waste processing that serves Philadelphia. The full Council still must act on the remaining contracts before they can take effect.

Inflation changes the comparison on Philadelphia Thanksgiving dinner prices

A local analysis says the long-term increase in holiday meal prices looks different once inflation is taken into account.

WHYY examined a new report from Philadelphia City Controller Christy Brady on Thanksgiving meal prices and found that while nominal costs have climbed sharply since 1976, the inflation-adjusted picture is more mixed. The analysis challenges the simpler impression that holiday dinner has become straightforwardly unaffordable over five decades.

The piece arrives as households continue to juggle grocery costs and interest-rate pressure. It also highlights how headline comparisons across long spans can obscure what inflation has done to purchasing power.

Space

NASA says Terra’s long Earth-observing mission is nearing its end

The satellite has spent nearly three decades tracking the planet’s atmosphere, land, oceans and ecosystems.

NASA said this week that its Terra mission will stop collecting science data in December, bringing to a close one of the agency’s longest-running Earth-observing satellite programs. Terra has operated for 27 years, helping scientists track wildfires, pollution, clouds, land changes and other linked systems.

The end of the mission marks a generational shift in Earth observation: some of its work has already been taken up by newer satellites, but Terra’s continuity made it one of the foundational records for climate and environmental monitoring.
