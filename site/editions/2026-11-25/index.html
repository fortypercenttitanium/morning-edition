U.S. prepares new tariff action against China, EU and others

The Trump administration is expected to announce higher tariffs aimed at what officials describe as subsidized overproduction abroad, according to people familiar with the plan.

The Trump administration is preparing to raise tariffs on goods from China, the European Union and 14 other economies, according to two people familiar with the matter who spoke to The Associated Press. The expected move would widen a trade fight that has continued even after Republicans’ midterm losses, with the White House arguing that foreign subsidies are distorting manufacturing competition and worsening strains on U.S. industry.

The prospective action arrives as inflation remains elevated and affordability has become a dominant political issue. Any tariff increase would risk higher import costs for some businesses and consumers while also signaling that the administration intends to keep using trade penalties as an industrial-policy tool. China has been trying to rebalance its economy, but weak domestic demand has pushed more firms toward export markets, adding to friction with trading partners.

The party’s margin is its largest in the chamber since 1990, setting up divided government in Washington.

Update Philadelphia advances bill requiring long-term shift away from incinerators and landfills

City Council’s commerce committee backed legislation calling for 60% of city trash to be diverted over 20 years.

Philadelphia would have to steer 60% of its trash away from landfills and incinerators over the next 20 years under legislation advanced Monday by City Council’s Commerce Committee. The bill directs the city to develop a formal zero-waste plan over the next three years, including expanded use of composting and other disposal methods.

The measure follows last week’s committee vote blocking the Parker administration from entering a new contract with Reworld, the Chester incinerator that has long handled roughly a third of the city’s trash. Administration officials said the new bill largely codifies work already underway, while some environmental advocates argued it needs stronger accountability and more emphasis on reducing waste at the source.

The agency tied the contrast to a very strong El Niño as the 2026 season nears its official end.
