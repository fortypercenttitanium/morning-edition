Space

NASA's twin ESCAPADE spacecraft complete Earth flybys en route to Mars

The mission used a loitering trajectory and Earth gravity assist to continue its trip toward studying Mars' magnetosphere.

NASA said its twin ESCAPADE spacecraft have completed powered Earth flybys and are now on the way to Mars. The mission launched in November 2025, spent time in an Earth-proximity loiter orbit and then used Earth's gravity to help redirect the spacecraft outward, an approach that also gave scientists opportunities to collect observations in Earth's magnetotail along the way.

This is the sort of mission that can sound remote until you remember what it represents: cheaper, more flexible deep-space operations and better understanding of how solar wind interacts with planetary atmospheres and magnetic environments.

Why it matters: Space missions often double as technology demonstrations, shaping how future robotic and human exploration is planned and funded.
